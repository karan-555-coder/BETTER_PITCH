Writing a call script your AI agent can follow

BetterPitch team · 7 min read

Your best receptionist doesn't read from a script. They know the business, ask the right questions and know when to get the owner. The goal is to write that knowledge down so an agent can do the same.

1. Start with the outcome

For each type of call, decide what a good result looks like: a booked appointment, a qualified lead, a message with a callback time. The agent should always be steering towards that result.

2. List the questions you always ask

Write down the three to five questions you need answered before you can quote or book. Keep them in the order a person would naturally ask them.

Example for a pest-control business: What kind of pest? Where in the home? How long has it been happening? Is it a house or an apartment? What area are you in?

3. Write down your rules

  • Which areas do you serve, and which don't you?
  • Which jobs are emergencies that should be transferred right away?
  • Which prices can be shared on the phone, and which need a visit?
  • When should the caller be passed to a person?

4. Collect your FAQs

Look at your last month of calls and emails. The same ten questions probably come up again and again. Write a short, plain answer to each one.

5. Test it like a customer

Call the agent yourself. Be vague, interrupt, change your mind, ask something odd. Every time it stumbles, add a line to the instructions. Most agents get noticeably better in the first week just from this.
